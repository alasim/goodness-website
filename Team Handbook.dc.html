G Goodness Society TEAM HANDBOOK

How Goodness operates — and where it takes us.

We are not building an NGO website. We are building the operating system for organized goodness — the digital infrastructure of a social movement. This handbook explains the machine step by step: how a person becomes impact, how money becomes evidence, and how one chapter becomes a country.

This is what goodness looks like when we organize it.
01 · THE NORTH STAR

Verified Goodness Created

Every team decision optimises one metric: goodness that actually happened and can be proven. Not registrations, not posts, not promises — verified people, verified hours, measured outcomes, documented money, growing network.

One truth
Every number on every page is computed from records — never typed into a box. If a stat is wrong, we fix the record, not the stat.
Trust is the product
Public drill-down from every taka to its documents; evidence attached to every published claim; nobody silently rewrites history.
Dignity in the data
People are never points. No public leaderboards, no beneficiaries as scores, no donor amounts exposed without consent.

03 · HOW WE OPERATE, STEP BY STEP

The operating loop

Eight steps, one cycle. Everything in the platform exists to run this loop faster, safer, and at larger scale.

1
A person joins
Someone discovers a mission or chapter and applies in 30 seconds. HQ reviews the application in Mission Control → People. Approval creates their Goodness ID and Goodness Passport — the verified identity that follows them for life: missions, hours, skills, journey level, credentials.
Tools: Volunteer (apply) · Mission Control → People · Volunteer Profile (passport)
2
Missions go live
Work is organised as Programs → Projects → Missions with concrete roles and capacity: “Teach 30 students, Mirpur, Saturday — 2 trainers + 1 photographer, 5/6 filled.” Chapters self-publish low-risk missions; HQ approves anything sensitive. Volunteers claim one primary role; urgent missions are flagged, never faked.
Tools: Missions board · Mission Detail · Mission Control → Missions · Chapter Control
3
Work is done — and verified
On-site volunteers check in by QR; remote volunteers start their contribution. After the work: Joined → Checked in → Contribution submitted → Hours verified. A team lead verifies every submission — only then do hours post to the Passport. Unverified hours never count anywhere.
Tools: Mission Detail (check-in) · Mission Control → Attendance · My Goodness → My missions
4
We measure what changed
Delivering is not the finish line. Each initiative produces an Impact Record: outputs vs target, outcomes with a basis label (Verified / Self-reported / Observed / Awaiting measurement), and evidence items our team checks. Records stay in draft until HQ publishes; the public page shows published records only. We say “29 secured employment” — never “we trained 120, mission accomplished.”
Tools: Mission Control → Impact · Impact (public)
5
Money moves in the open
Every taka — donation, member commitment, or partner funding — enters the Trust Ledger with a receipt, flows to a fund, and leaves as an expense with documents attached. HQ approves expenses in Mission Control; the public can drill from total received down to a single invoice. Secured (signed commitments) is never blurred with received (cash in hand).
Tools: Trust Ledger (public) · Mission Control → Money & approvals · Fund Impact (marketplace)
6
Partners join the work
The pipeline runs enquiry → proposal → committed → active → renewal. Every active partner gets a Partner Room: committed/received/deployed, the people delivering the work, employee volunteering, and a live trace of their contribution. Results are pooled across all funders — shared attribution, never claimed twice. Renewal is a continuation, not a reset.
Tools: Partner (builder) · Partner Room · Partner Profile (public) · Mission Control → Partners
7
Chapters multiply it
Decentralised execution, central governance. Anyone can propose a chapter; HQ approves it into forming, then active once Goodness Standards are met. Chapter leads run local missions and rosters in Chapter Control and propose budgets to HQ. Every chapter number is a filter over the same records — chapters never keep separate counters, and health comparisons stay HQ-private.
Tools: Chapters · Chapter page · Chapter Control · Mission Control → Network
8
Every verified moment recruits the next person
The Share Studio turns real events into evidence-backed cards — milestones, credentials, impact updates, funding progress, chapter launches. Verified numbers are locked (a Goodness card is a social claim backed by the platform), and the OS surfaces the moment itself: “Worth sharing now” in Mission Control, share chips on the Passport, commitment panel, chapters, missions, and Partner Rooms.
Tools: Share Studio · Mission Control → Worth sharing now
The flywheel: person acts → Goodness verifies → the moment becomes shareable → friends discover Goodness → some volunteer, some commit, some companies partner → more missions happen → more verified moments. Round and round.
04 · WHO DOES WHAT

Roles and their powers

Volunteer
Joins missions, checks in, submits contributions, tracks everything in My Goodness, shares milestones. Sees own Passport grow.
Team Lead
Runs the mission on the ground and verifies attendance — the human checkpoint every hour passes through.
Chapter Lead
Runs the local roster and missions in Chapter Control, proposes budgets and missions to HQ, keeps standards moving toward 6/6.
HQ (Mission Control)
The command centre. Approves applications, expenses, impact publishing, credentials, partner stages, chapter lifecycle. The Decision Inbox is the daily queue; every action lands in the audit trail.
Member
Sustains the movement with a Goodness Commitment; may pause, resume, or change any time — recognition never downgrades.
Partner
Backs initiatives and watches the work live in the Partner Room; chooses whether funding details are public or partner-only.
05 · HOW WE SPEAK

Language discipline

Our credibility lives in our wording. These rules apply everywhere — pages, cards, captions, reports.

Never: “We created 29 jobs”
Always: “29 secured employment following Career Launch — outcome basis: verified”
Never: “৳500 = one life changed”
Always: pooled-contribution framing — results are shared across all funders, never sold per unit
Never: “lives touched”, failure language on missed targets
Always: one vocabulary — “people supported”, and the honest % on targets still in progress
Never: a typed number on a share card or report
Always: numbers pulled live from records, locked against editing, marked Goodness Verified ✓
06 · HOW WE RUN THE WEEK

Operating cadence

The OS tells you what needs you — the cadence is when you look. No spreadsheets, no status meetings about things a screen already answers.

EVERY DAY
Drive the Decision Inbox to zero: applications, attendance verification, expenses, enquiries, chapter proposals. A submission never waits more than 24 hours — speed of response is part of the product.
EVERY FRIDAY
Post from Worth sharing now (the This Week digest writes itself), review chapter health in Network command, nudge any chapter with no live mission, check the partner pipeline for stalled proposals.
EVERY MONTH
Publish ready impact records (evidence first), share the This Month digest, review funding gaps in the marketplace, and walk the Trust Ledger as a visitor would — if anything confuses you, it confuses a donor.
EVERY QUARTER
Partner quarterly briefs from the Partner Room, open renewal conversations early (a renewal is a continuation, not a reset), review chapter standards progress, revisit programme targets against the year goal.
07 · DECISION RULES

Who decides what — and what “done” means

Chapters decide alone
Low-risk local missions, rosters, local partner conversations, chapter share cards. Move fast — it's your city.
HQ must approve
Budgets and expenses, impact publishing, credentials, partner commitments, chapter lifecycle changes. Propose in Chapter Control; HQ answers within a day.
Nobody, ever
Typing a stat by hand, editing a verified number, exposing private data, claiming sole credit for pooled results, deleting history instead of reversing it.
A missionis done when contributions are submitted and a team lead verified the hours — not when the event ends.
Impactis real when the record is published with evidence attached and each outcome carries its basis label — not when we delivered.
Moneyis spent when the expense is approved with documents on the ledger — a payment without paper is a pending item, not a fact.
A partnershipis active when the commitment is captured with amount and type and its Partner Room is live — a handshake is a proposal.
08 · NEW HERE?

Your first week on the team

Learn the machine by living each side of it. By Friday you'll have been a visitor, a volunteer, a funder, and an operator.

DAY 2Live the volunteer loop: sign in from the directory in My Goodness, join a mission role, check in, submit a contribution, watch it wait for verification.
DAY 3Operate: open Mission Control, verify that attendance, watch the hours land on the Passport, then follow one expense from pending to approved on the public ledger.
DAY 4See the capital side: Fund Impact as a donor, a Partner Room as a sponsor, a chapter page and Chapter Control as a chapter lead.
DAY 5Close the loop: open Share Studio, create your identity card, and read GOODNESS-OS.md — the master plan this handbook summarises. Bring one question and one improvement idea to the team.
